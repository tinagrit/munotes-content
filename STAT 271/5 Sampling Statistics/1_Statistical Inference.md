## Statistical Inference
Statistics [[1_Statistics and Data#Population and data|uses sample data]] to make **inference** about an entire population.

![[chart01.png|400]]

We assume that the values of the **individuals** in a population are ***iid*.** (*independent and identically distributed*) in the underlying **population probability distribution**.

The population probability distribution can be:
1. **Specified** with some unknown parameters, a [[#Parametric Inference|parametric inference]] problem
2. **Unknown**, a non-parametric inference problem

### Parametric Inference
When a distribution can be suggested for the population, the **sample data** will be used to **estimate the parameters** to the suggested distribution.

A random sample of size $n$ is a set of *iid*. random variables $X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n}$, having a common distribution $F$.

A [[2_Common Statistics#Common Statistics|statistic]] is a [[2_Random Variables#Random Variables|random variable]] whose value is determined **by the sample data**. The most important statistics are the [[2_Common Statistics#Sample mean|sample mean]] and the [[2_Common Statistics#Sample variance|sample variance]].
